-HomeFront-

Washington D.C. - Overnight the Department of Agriculture reported a large-scale cyberattack, which took their systems offline this week. Various USDA websites remained offline for some time, with the main frontend disruptions being reported in the portals used to manage grants and insurance policies. The USDA stated that the cyberattack exploited a vulnerability which resulted in IT departments having to reset all passwords in all server environments, and as a result, further hiccups and disruptions might take place over the next few days.

Separately, National Cybersecurity Director Sean Cairncross was dismissed yesterday afternoon, with his replacement being named as current Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies. This position is responsible for advising the President on all cybersecurity issues.

Iowa: Today's detonation of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant has been delayed, after public outrage over the blast causing widespread lead contamination. The controlled detonation will now take place on Monday, after more preparations can be undertaken by locals to protect against lead exposure.

Analyst Comment: Staying indoors on blast day is one of the primary means of protecting against lead exposure, but agricultural efforts will still be affected by the detonation; there are a lot of corn fields in the zone expected to be contaminated with lead, which has caused concerns for next years crop as the lead dust will be plowed into the soil during normal farming operations. Schools also remain closed and government agencies recommend children to stay indoors for some time, as the lead dust from the explosive compound is expected to settle over the area.
