Analyst Comment: Within Israel, the vote count continued overnight, with no clear winner emerging by morning. Current vote counts as of this afternoon indicate that neither Netanyahu nor Eisenkot have received the majority needed to form a government without a coalition.

Based on the official vote count so far, this is looking like a de facto victory for Netanyahu, even though his party is likely going to lose the majority lead they previously held. In Parliamentary systems around the world, parties which fail to get a majority, must form a coalition government with another party, counting that party's votes toward the total. In short, if a party can't clear 50%, they will have to join together with a smaller party to bump them up into the majority. Normally, when coalition governments are formed, political deals are made: the smaller party gets positions in government (and thus more power), and the larger party gets to form the majority of government. In this case, the margin is razor thin even a day after the polls closed. In the short term, the election is not likely to result in much immediate change, at least not within the window of potential escalation that's on the menu over the next few weeks. If neither party is able to secure a majority, Netanyahu remains Prime Minister for several more months, as some form of coalition is formed. If no agreement can be achieved, the election itself will be run once again next spring and the process starts all over again, with Netanyahu remaining Prime Minister in the meantime.
