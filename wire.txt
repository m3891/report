-International Events-

Germany: Overnight a cyberattack breached the SAP corporation, which resulted in some of their code being compromised. The enterprise software giant confirmed that malign actors had gained access to their GitHub, and pushed malicious code which resulted in unknown impact. SAP has stated that none of the malicious code made it into the software they provide to customers, however the investigation is ongoing.

United Kingdom: This morning a cyberattack resulted in government pension services going down for many citizens. The Department for Work and Pensions confirmed that their payment systems were compromised by malign actors, but payments to pensioners and welfare recipients were not affected. Benefit recipients could not access their accounts or access the administrative side of programs during the outage.

Separately, customers of the Lloyd's banking group and their subsidiary Bank of Scotland reported issues accessing their banking data this morning, with many customers stating that they could not make payments, whereas other customers reported no issues. No further information was provided regarding this outage at the time of this report.

-HomeFront-

Maryland: USCYBERCOM is investigating a breach of the U.S. Air Force software suite Cloud One, the cloud computing ecosystem used to manage a variety of IT systems. The scope and nature of the breach has not been disclosed, and officials have only stated that the breach was discovered during routine maintenance operations last month.

Analyst Comment: This breach is a big deal as Cloud One is a foundational system that supports both classified and unclassified systems. It's the bedrock of Air Force data and IT management needs which supports everything from payroll software, to classified targeting data and the radar systems used by NORAD to defend North America's airspace.

Analyst Comment: In the Middle East, things are slowly heating up once again. Houthi strikes continue to devastate Saudi oil infrastructure regularly, to the point that global markets don't even react anymore, despite major refineries shutting down due to the strikes. Iranian forces continue their daily targeting of commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz, and the Omani coastline has become a graveyard of stricken ships undergoing repairs after being hit while trying to make the crossing. The American military posture remains steady throughout the region, though the effects of the midterm elections on wartime ideations seem to have stifled the potential for escalation, at least for the moment.

Iranian strikes have continued to not just target merchant shipping, but also land-based installations as well. Kuwait and Bahrain have been struck several times over the past week by Iranian missiles and drones, with strikes now regularly taking place throughout Kuwait City. Last night two Italian contractors were killed in Kuwait after the drone countermeasure system they were working on was struck by a drone. Notably, while Iran claims credit for the other strikes in Kuwait City, they explicitly deny the strike that killed the two Italians, with IRGC leadership claiming that this strike was a false-flag operation. This could be a lie intended to reduce tensions after Iran mistakenly killed citizens of a third-party nation, or it could be a false flag operation as they claim. Iran has not shied away from claiming credit for strikes in the past, but information shaping operations are a constant of the war, and the truth is always hard to verify. Either way, missiles and drone strikes are now a regular nightly feature around the region, and right now no diplomatic solutions are anywhere near being on the table for the time being.
