-International Events-

Spain: Following yesterday's IED explosion in Oviedo, the lead suspect remains at large. The early details of what caused the initial incident are also not clear; some locals claim that it was domestic dispute that resulted in the initial shooting. The victim has not been identified (nor her relationship to the shooter) but she is expected to make a full recovery.

Analyst Comment: The involvement of the IED also remains a mystery, but due to the short time frame between the initial incident and the blast, the device would have had to be pre-built before everything kicked off. Organized crime elements in Spain are known to use explosives occasionally, but this case has more questions than answers, and very few details have been provided by local authorities.

Saudi Arabia: Houthi targeting efforts have expanded over the past few days. Several pumping stations along the East-West Pipeline were struck two days ago, and more strikes were reported overnight. Similar strikes were reported targeting Saudi oil refineries, causing unknown damage.

Italy: This morning a large-scale series of bomb threats were called in at schools around the country. Over 300x primary schools were evacuated this morning, after an anonymous user emailed explosive threats. Two days ago, a similar series of emails was sent to administrators at 280x schools around the country. So far, all of these threats have been deemed to be non-viable, and business resumed as usual after security checks.

-HomeFront-

Washington D.C. - Last night, the DoJ announced the indictment of three Iranian nationals on charges of conspiring to conduct terror attacks throughout the United States and Europe. Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi, Mohammed Ali Al-Hassani, and Mohsen Mohajeri were indicted for their alleged involvement in the arson attacks on synagogues in the United Kingdom, as well as in the planning phases of the alleged incident at RAF Fairford airbase back in September.

-----END TEARLINE-----

Analyst Comment: Last night's indictments are interesting for a few different reasons, and the document itself doesn't really clear up much. Based on the press release, it appears as though many different previous attacks are the main focus of the DoJ, with the link to Fairford tacked on to the case at the end. The DoJ is alleging that these three guys were the masterminds behind the alleged Fairford operation, though the actual raw text of the indictment itself make that correlation questionable based on what is already known regarding that case.

Most of the indictment is a copy/paste of Al-Saadi's earlier indictment, which details his long history of violent ideations and attack planning. Al-Saadi was initially arrested back in May of this year, on a laundry list of terrorism charges, which the earlier indictment outlined very clearly. A lot of the evidence presented in the document suggests Al-Saadi was the main planner of the earlier HAYI attacks on synagogues in the United Kingdom a few months ago, in addition to vandalism and more violent attack planning. This is what resulted in his initial discovery and arrest back in May. This more recent indictment tacks on the Fairford incident, and adds in two more suspects....both of which are are current IRGC officials located within Iran.

As it stands, the DoJ is claiming that the Fairford incident was a legitimate terror attack, and that the masterminds are a man who was already in jail on other charges, and two IRGC members located in Iran. The "Fairford Five", the individuals actually on the ground and carrying out the alleged attack (though somehow not having any explosives or going anywhere near a main gate), are still on bail...and no charges have been filed. It's rather odd to indict the masterminds, but not the actual militants themselves, so there are still a lot of unanswered questions regarding this case and whether or not the Fairford incident was a real attack. Regardless, someone clearly thinks genuine attacks are still planned.

Two days ago, the US shuffled around more aircraft at British bases...right around the same time these indictments were unsealed. As there would be no reason to move aircraft otherwise, there's a high probability that some unknown threat still remains. Adding more charges to a man already in jail, and two Iranian nationals that are back home is not likely to reduce the current unspecified threats to American bases in the region.
