-International Events-

Canada: Saturday afternoon, a mass casualty incident was declared at Canadian Forces Base (CFB) Wainwright, following a major incident that occurred during training. At approximately 4:00pm local time, a wildfire broke out in the training area at roughly the same time a strong outflow boundary was approaching the base. Within a matter of minutes, the resulting high winds caused the fire to shift direction toward hundreds of service members taking part in training operations. 

Due to the catastrophic nature of the fire, the true casualty count is unknown. A total of 4x fatalities have been confirmed, however more victims are expected. 39x others were injured, 19x of which are in critical care.

Analyst Comment: The circumstances of the incident's origin are unclear, however claims have been made by eyewitnesses on the base disputing the origin of the fire. Officially, the cause of the fire is under investigation and has not yet been disclosed. However the press statements presented by officials have been extremely carefully worded to due to the ongoing investigations. Whatever the cause of the incident (either a training accident or a genuine wildfire situation) decisions were made which lead to the deaths of Canadian soldiers, so the investigation into what happened will take time.

France: Riots continued to escalate over the weekend, as arson attacks and states of unrest remained constant in most major cities throughout the nation. Many government buildings and structures have been set alight, and looting has become commonplace in many cities due to the unrest. This afternoon, curfews were emplaced for the Republique and Châtelet districts of Paris, as more rioting is expected throughout the next few days.

Albania: Mass riots have continued throughout the nation after the Supreme Court ruled that Jared Kushner's luxury resort project on Sazan Island can go forward. Protests began last week and have been intensifying for several days, before reaching a flashpoint over the weekend, which was spurred on by Prime Minister Rama refusing to resign. Yesterday the Supreme Court was burned down, after rioters breached the compound, and set the building on fire.

Analyst Comment: This situation has been brewing for a while, and has been a major focus for Albanians for several months. The Kushner project on Sazan has been a major source of both protests, but also corruption concerns. Locals claim that their Prime Minister has been paid off, and that he's selling off major national real estate for personal gain. As a result, these riots will be worthy of monitoring more closely due to the potential for instability to spread throughout the Balkans.

Middle East: Travel warnings throughout the region continue, as US Embassies in nearly the entire Middle East have issued more pressing travel advisories warning of an imminent increase in wartime conditions. Other than the United States, the diplomatic missions of most other western-aligned nations have issued similar warnings, indicating that preparations are being made for the war to expand at some point.

-HomeFront-

Washington D.C. - Early this morning a shooting was reported on 8th Street SE, which resulted in a National Guard soldier being wounded. Local authorities state that a street fight erupted in front of the Engine Company 18 Firehouse just before midnight. A vehicle containing a squad of National Guard soldiers was parked on the street at the time the fight broke out, with the soldiers exiting the vehicle to stop the fight. Immediately after the squad exited the vehicle, one suspect produced a firearm, which resulted in a small arms engagement in street, with three soldiers returning fire during the skirmish. One soldier sustained a gunshot wound to the leg, and the suspect was also shot during the engagement.

Analyst Comment: Many of the riots underway around the world are the real deal, and appear to be transitioning into more of a protracted state of global civil unrest. However, most of the reasons for the rioting vary widely between nations. In short, the various states of unrest around the world are summarized as follows:

France: Student/economic protests turned riot, large migrant communes looting.
Belgium: Student protests turned riot, inspired by French riots.
Albania: Kushner/Rama protests turned anti-corruption riots.
Argentina: Student protests turned anti-Milei/anti-corruption riots.
Brazil: Riots over election integrity after Bolsonaro refuses to resign.
Mexico: Riots due to drinking water contamination in Guadalajara.
Italy: Riots due to rising fuel costs, some student riots similar to France.

France is the epicenter for much of the unrest, and the development and motivations for the French riots are complicated. The origin of the riots is multi-fold; these events started as student protests regarding conditions in schools, as well as more general economic concerns. At the same time, there was an element of children from second and third-generation migrant families, which were protesting government spending being cut for them in schools.

These protests started under relatively low-level social terms, but after the first day this initial spark rapidly transitioned (within a matter of hours) into a series of much larger issues, and now, several different factions are participating in the fray. What started small has turned out the be a pressure relief valve for tensions that have been boiling for weeks. Students are engaging in bedlam in most areas, migrant communities are looting whatever they can, pro-Palestine and ANTIFA activists are conducting their own riots for their own reasons, and now, many regular French citizens are protesting as well due to the high costs of fuel brought on by the war in the Middle East. Perhaps the clearest view of the types of demographics rioting, are the raw, unfiltered videos from the riots themselves. Many people are emotionally and politically invested in avoiding the migration element to the looting and violence throughout the nation. Many videos indicate a lot rioters are of African descent, and even for those who seek to be as neutral as possible, it is challenging to find videos (not still images that can be more easily selectively framed, but raw videos) which do not feature individuals of non-French heritage. To be sure, there are a lot of ethnic French citizens engaging in the original protests that started this all, and many are indeed rioting as well. Determining exactly how much of the unrest is local unrest vs. migrant unrest will take time, as the situation is incredibly nuanced and there are certainly interests on all sides which seek to scapegoat their adversaries while also downplaying other demographics. As of this evening, this is even more complicated as pro-police demonstrators have gathered in certain areas to protest against the riots (protests which have remained low-intensity gatherings for now).

In terms of the riots in other nations that are coordinated with the French riots, the unrest in Belgium and Italy are most similar to the unrest that is underway in France. Belgium popped off around the same time as France, due to a lot of cross-border traffic allowing easy access to major metropolitan areas outside France. The riots in Italy are also somewhat related, however Italy's problems mostly have arisen more directly from the economic realm, as agricultural protests regarding fuel prices began a few days ago, which presented the distractions necessary for looters to break into stores. Most of the looting appears to have taken place a decent distance from the farmer protests, unlike in France which has presented the old standby tactic of "peaceful protesters" starting off just fine during the day, but putting on masks and breaking things once night falls. Within Argentina, Brazil, Albania, Mexico, and other nations currently experiencing unrest, riots are being conducted for their own reasons, which vary by locale.

Timing wise, many are speculating that it's odd for all of these protests to come about on the same weekend. However this is not true; Mexico's riots have been brewing for months, with several smaller riots taking place earlier this month. France's protests have been ongoing for weeks, and Albania's protests have been constant for several months. It would be worthwhile to examine whether or not some of these riots are being funded more directly, however at this point it's going to be a case-by-case evaluation which is not going to be easy. What is certain, is that within France the riots will continue for some time, and likely grow in severity as the main elements driving the chaos (the migration situation, and the energy crisis) remain unaddressed.
