-International Events-

Ukraine: President Trump claimed that Russia and Ukraine have agreed to an "energy ceasefire", to halt the targeting of petroleum infrastructure. Neither Russia nor Ukraine have publicly verified this claim, and Ukraine has stated that any ceasefire depends on Russia to reciprocate.

Poland: This morning one arrest was made regarding what is being described as an assassination attempt on Prime Minister Donald Tusk, as well as the Polish Defense Minister. The nature of the alleged plot has not been disclosed, and the identity of the suspect has not yet been released.

Analyst Comment: The details of this case are not clear, as authorities have not stated whether or not the assailant had taken physical action to conduct an attack. So far, all official statements have indicated that a 60-year-old man made threats against the Prime Minister on social media, but no physical act of violence (or preparation for such) has been noted so far.

Saudi Arabia: Over the weekend, Houthi targeting continued with more strikes on Saudi airports. On Saturday, Ansar Allah (the Houthis) launched one ballistic missile toward King Khalid International Airport, with the missile directly impacting Terminal 3. Video footage from the time of impact confirms that no warning was given, and the terminal was packed with travelers at the time of the strike. As a result, over 300x people were wounded and 12x people were killed instantly. The next day, Ansar Allah struck Terminal 4 in a similar manner.

Saturday night, missile strikes were also reported at King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, with unknown success. Separately, a total of three gas and petroleum facilities were also struck, including the Ghawar Oil Fields, the Shedgum Gas Complex, and the Tanajib Oil and Gas Complex.

Strait of Hormuz: Strikes on merchant shipping continued to escalate over the past 48 hours. Several ships were struck over the weekend with a variety of munitions, and strikes continued throughout the day within the Strait itself, as well as the Gulf of Oman.

Analyst Comment: At this point, there are so many ships getting struck daily in the Strait of Hormuz that it's challenging to confirm each one. The UKMTO has mostly been withholding reporting for several days, and only releasing "time late" reports at least a day after a strike takes place. The United States did strike one tanker over the weekend, however Iran has been striking several ships per day for about two weeks.

-HomeFront-

Gulf Coast: Due to the disruptions caused by the arrival of Hurricane Isaias, the Department of Energy authorized an emergency release of 4 million barrels of crude from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR). This latest release will take the SPR below 279 million barrels in the Reserve all total, minus whatever crude is drained as part of the 40 million barrel exchange that was authorized last week.

Washington D.C. - Yesterday the Department of Homeland Security conducted a raid at the residence of Rep. Ilhan Omar as part of an ongoing investigation.

Analyst Comment: No details or acknowledgement of the raid has been provided by officials regarding what this was about, however based on recent statements by the White House this could be related to the years-long allegations that she married her brother to secure his greencard status, or her own immigration status. More details are expected later on.

Analyst Comments: The escalation of targeting in Saudi Arabia is incredibly serious, and is starting to bring about long-term problems for the Kingdom. In short, Mohammad bin Salman (MBS) is in trouble, and if the Houthi targeting keeps up (and civilians keep getting killed), his inability to protect his nation is starting the threaten the throne itself. Missile strikes are bad enough, but ever since the East-West Pipeline was first taken offline by Houthi missiles a few weeks ago, the very GDP of the nation itself is at risk. Due to their main source of national income being mostly offline, two weeks ago Saudi Arabia requested a series of emergency loans totaling roughly $8 billion USD from various banks, just to keep the lights on in the Kingdom. The economic status of the House of Saud has always been a complicated affair, but generally speaking for a nation that is built upon a financial bedrock of crude....things aren't going so well.

Ansar Allah expanding their targeting efforts to include airports is (unfortunately for Saudi Arabia) a very effective means of both taking the fight to their enemy's capital city, but also in exerting some ability to impact airspace thousands of miles away from the front lines on the ground. This morning a Notice to Airmen (NOTAM) was issued for the majority of Saudi airspace, warning of continued fighting, and that the security situation presents risk to aircraft at all altitudes. Also, King Khalid airport remains fully closed for at least a week due to the airstrikes.

Though the strikes around the region have been desensitizing after almost 8 months of war, the inability for Saudi Arabia to protect their own capital city is extremely significant for the political situation in the Kingdom. MBS has been unable to protect his nation's income source, and now is incapable of protecting the airport where his family keeps their private jets. If this keeps up, the House of Saud is going to face some very serious threats, not just from outside the nation...but from within. Throughout the war, MBS himself has reportedly been physically traveling between bunker facilities each night, and even though Saudi Arabia is not likely to experience regime change anytime soon, that possibility remains on the table as the tensions within government keep escalating with each Houthi missile.
