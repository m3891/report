-International Events-

France: This morning authorities have issued blanket bans on protests in some areas, due to the potential for last weekend's unrest to be repeated. All protests have been banned throughout the entire city of Lyon, and within Paris gatherings are prohibited in some districts as the curfews stay in place. Most major cities are banning protests unless conducted by unions and registered with the government.

Middle East: Late-stage preparations regarding a return to the shooting phase of the war continue to mount. Last night, State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott attempted to fact-check a post on social media regarding personnel changes in the region, but in doing so he directly confirmed that State Department employees and their families are being evacuated from Saudi Arabia. Within the Strait of Hormuz, Iranian targeting efforts continue regularly. Yesterday, Iran struck a total of 4x tanker vessels, none of which have been confirmed by the UKMTO yet. 

Analyst Comment: On the rhetorical front, the situation is not looking good. Statements made by White House officials (and President Trump himself) indicate that a preemptive strike on Iran is possibly imminent. Last night while standing on the tarmac preparing to board Air Force One, President Trump directly stated that negotiations with Iran are not ongoing, and heavily implied that strikes will be taking place immediately after the midterms. Within Israel, PM Netanyahu has begun an intensive media campaign, stating that their intelligence indicates that Iran has decided to build a nuclear weapon, and that "there is only one way to remove this threat once and for all, to topple the regime in Iran, and that is my directive to the IDF".

Analyst Comment: Around the world, the cybersecurity incidents of the past few days continue to expand in severity. In short, this week is certainly one for the history books for the IT world. Regarding the PHP vulnerability, the NSA, FBI, CISA, most of Europe, and the Prime Minister of Japan have offered up two solutions, and two solutions only...immediately update servers, or unplug them completely. This applies to roughly 70% of the entire internet, and some estimates claim that roughly 35% of all websites are unpatchable as they run on a version of PHP which is too old to be fixed. Within a few days, up to a third of the entire internet might be gone forever, if those web servers are not manually rebuilt from scratch. Yesterday, thousands of websites went offline, to include the government websites of France, the Netherlands, Finland, Japan, and dozens of others relying on PHP. Everything from Wikipedia to home media servers are affected, and manual intervention is needed in order for most internet-connected servers to be fixed.

More broadly, many professionals are calling it "Black October" due to the severity and the impact of the events that have unfolded over the past few days. In addition to the PHP vulnerability and the eMudhra saga, around a dozen other completely separate issues have popped up, nearly all of which would have grabbed headlines on their own. Most of these vulnerabilities were in place a decade ago, and are only being discovered (or exploited) right now, a short time before major elections in Israel and the United States. The nature of the problem itself also makes it worse: Unfortunately, a significant portion of the world depends on random servers running in someone's dusty closet since 2009, and since the solution right now is patch it or unplug it, the true impact of this will not be known until more bad things happen.

To say that the IT world is freaking out would be putting it mildly, even though the full scale of what's happened will take weeks to determine. In terms of real-world impact, most normal people won't see the severity of what's happened (or even be aware of it at all) with the exception of various websites going down for a short time. The real damage will surface months from now, after malign actors have time to exploit whatever vulnerabilities existed, but weren't fixed fast enough.
