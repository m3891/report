-International Events-

Europe: This morning the European Commission revealed being the victim of a cyberattack back in October. Internal Human Resources networks, payroll data, and employee medical disbursement information was breached, with these systems remaining offline for roughly ten days. This breach was only disclosed last night.

Saudi Arabia: This morning Houthi attacks continued to strike Riyadh, with several drone impacts being reported throughout the city. The Public Investment Fund (PIF) Tower was struck by a Houthi drone in Riyadh's financial district, and several other smaller buildings were also hit on the outskirts of town. Similar strikes were reported in Jubail, King Khalid airport, and an airbase.

Analyst Comment: These strikes were rather embarrassing for the Saudis, as the PIF tower is the tallest building in Riyadh and is a major point of prestige for the Kingdom. The continual targeting by Ansar Allah (Houthis) is a persistent problem that multiple Patriot, THAAD, and Hawk missile defense sites don't seem to be capable of defending against.

-HomeFront-

Iowa: The US Army released a public advisory for a controlled detonation of hazardous materials at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown. At noon tomorrow, 68x drums of lead azide that are too hazardous to move will be detonated on site, which is expected to create hazardous conditions for locals up to 100 miles north of the blast site. The dust cloud of lead is expected to contaminate the local area, and locals are advised to stay indoors and turn off air conditioning.

Analyst Comment: In the Middle East, reports emerged last night indicating the USS *GEORGE WASHINGTON* CSG has departed CENTCOM and is returning to her home port in Japan. Despite the US Navy saying that the Roosevelt would not be replacing the Washington, that appears to have been what's taken place.

The rotation of the third carrier back home is an indicator of de-escalation, however there's still more than enough firepower to carry out strikes in Iran once again. As a reminder, there were only two carriers in the Middle East when the war kicked off in the first place (the Ford and the Lincoln), so it's possible that this third carrier is assessed to not be needed, especially now that the Washington is pushing 6 months at sea with very challenging logistical conditions.

This movement might be better described in the context of the geopolitical developments in China. The trilateral meeting between Russia, China, and the United States appears to have gone well, with President Xi stating that the talks were of "groundbreaking significance", leading to hopes that the talks may have been fruitful in clearing the air regarding the global spheres of influence. What has not yet been discussed publicly in any meaningful manner, is the impact the summit had on the war in the Middle East.

Considering that both Russia and China are directly arming and assisting Iran, the impact of these talks on the war will be interesting to see...it may have no impact at all, or it may have been a moment of clarity that leads to an off-ramp. Neither Russia nor China probably want to urge a true end to the war, as they benefit from the US being bogged down to some degree. However the level of international instability is also causing them negative impact, so they probably seek to let the US putter along with no real solution (no hot war, but also no peace). Time will tell how this develops, and even with one carrier going home, we're not out of the woods just yet.
