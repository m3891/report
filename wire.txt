-International Events-

Europe: Throughout the continent, cyberattacks on critical infrastructure have continued. So far, a total of 11x nations have reported being impacted by cyberattacks targeting Internet-of-Things (IoT) devices such as internet connected refrigeration sensors. As of this morning, these vulnerabilities have expanded expanded beyond the initial impacts to supermarkets. Overnight, refrigerators at several blood banks in France were reportedly breached, with staff members at three different facilities taking emergency actions to save Fresh Whole Blood (FWB) units from being compromised.

Analyst Comment: Concerning the culprits of these attacks, no entity has been named. Romanian officials have offered up more details on the attacks in their country, stating that the attacks seemed amateurish in nature, and were probably not a nationstate actor. Considering the fallout of the PHPShell and other major cybersecurity incidents of the past few weeks, it's anyone's guess as to who's behind the recent wave of attacks.

-HomeFront-

Mississippi: This morning an explosion was reported at the Loveland Products plant in Greenville, which shut down the factory for some time. This facility is produces herbicides and fertilizers for agricultural purposes. The cause of the blast is not known, and the exact chemicals involved in the incident have not yet been disclosed. The fire was contained after a few hours, and the investigation continues.

USA: Around the nation, yesterday's midterm elections resulted in major gains for the Democrats, with most elections being a clean sweep in their favor. Vote counts are still being tallied but overnight estimates strongly indicate the Republicans lost the House and the Senate, with several key Governor spots flipping blue as well. In Washington, President Trump canceled most of his public appearances for the next week, though this afternoon he made some unplanned remarks at the site of the Ballroom construction project.

Analyst Comment: Now that the midterms are over, a major milestone is reached within the two major wars being fought overseas. Like it or not, the midterm election results were a major factor in planning the war in Iran, with the White House openly stating multiple times that the milestone of elections reflects a decision point. Now that the results are in, the situation can radically change. President Trump himself is no longer held back by the negative connotations in the polls, as the political situation can't get any worse now that the midterm damage is done. On the other hand, the massive defeat at the polls could cause a large strategic pivot away from the war to save his legacy.

Where this goes now is anyone's guess, but decisions made during a period of chaos often tend to not be the most well-thought out. Similarly, most assessments of the future really have to stretch to theorize what the potential courses of action might be, as the possibilities are extremely wide and varied. When stuck in such a situation, it is usually helpful to make decisions based on the tangible evidence that is certain.

In the case of what might happen in the next few days/weeks, it is certain that the United States has surged forces into the Middle East. Diplomats and their families have long been evacuated, and yesterday some districts in Israel opened up their public shelters amid high tensions in the region. The USS Theodore Roosevelt CSG is steaming for the Strait of Malacca, and the two carriers already on station are still cutting waves in the Arabian Sea.

On the diplomatic front, not much is known about whatever talks are taking place with Iran though intermediaries with the United States, but many Gulf states have sent diplomats to Tehran over the past week, probably to secure their own deals with Iran independently of the United States. Many different officials in many Arab nations have been told that the decision has already been made to return to the war, and that it's only a matter of time. Obviously hearsay among politicians is not something easily verifiable, but all Gulf States have been preparing for this possibility for weeks and since a lot of hardware is still heading into the region, military options are very much on the table.
