-International Events-

Black Sea: Attacks on merchant shipping continued yesterday afternoon with a drone attack targeting the M/T *GAS HUSKY* (IMO: 9507764) off the coast of Bulgaria. All crew were rescued unharmed but the cargo of propane ignited during the strikes, causing a very large fire for some time. At the time of the strike, the vessel was carrying cargo from Egypt to Romania.

Middle East: Iranian drone strikes were reported overnight, targeting American bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. The success of these strikes was not known, but impacts were noted by locals in Kuwait.

Analyst Comment: There are claims that some American service members were wounded during these strikes, and plane watchers did observe a medevac aircraft departing Germany last night, bound for the Middle East shortly after the strikes. However, there's no way to know if any casualties were sustained. CENTCOM flatly denies any injuries, but they have routinely been caught lying, specifically about wounded service members, so the truth will be hard to determine.

-HomeFront-

USA: Overnight, cyber incidents continued as before. This morning, a security vulnerability was discovered in Java, which has caused widespread concerns of more cybersecurity vulnerabilities. Oracle released a critical patch update overnight, with IT professionals working to harden their systems against the latest round of vulnerabilities.

Maryland: Last night a large-scale outage of the NSA's internal information sharing network was reported, with the NSANet system going offline for several hours.

Analyst Comment: This system functions as the NSA's intranet, and in terms of real-world use is easiest to describe as the NSA's own private version of the SIPRNET and JWICS, the networks used to share nearly all classified information. The NSA has stated that this outage was the result of a routine software update, however the details don't really add up...software updates don't normally take up half a workday, in the middle of a Tuesday. Whatever the truth is, will probably not be known due to the highly classified nature of this system.

Illinois: Yesterday evening the FBI announced the arrest of one suspect as part of the investigation into the IED attack on the BioUrja Renewables facility in Peoria last Friday. Andrew Kozak, a trainee at the facility, was arrested for threatening to blow up the facility.

Analyst Comment: At face value, the Peoria incident seems like a fairly routine open-and-shut case. However, this arrest is interesting and there may be more to the story than meets the eye. For one, the charges are currently sealed by a judge, so we don't have any substantial details of the case. Based on press statements and statements by FBI Director Kash Patel, Kozak has not been charged with any terrorism charges, he's only been charged with two counts of making threats. The very carefully worded statements by the FBI avoided directly attributing the incident to Kozak...they never explicitly state that he was the guy that planted the explosive device(s), they only say he was arrested for making threats. This is an incredibly important distinction that is easily missed, but is extremely critical to verify and confirm. Rioters who throw improvised fireworks at federal buildings are frequently hit with Weapons of Mass Destruction charges, and yet this guy, who reportedly built a legitimate IED that caused one of the largest evacuation zones in years, has only been charged with making threats. The critical factor in this case was the discovery of the IED itself. Local, state, and federal agencies adamantly stated that a real and genuine IED was discovered, and possibly several more were found as the search continued throughout the night.

Based on the press statements, either this discovery of an IED was a complete fabrication, and this entire saga was just a disgruntled employee sending a threatening email, or the suspect who sent a threatening email is not the same guy that planted a no-joke IED. None of the statements indicate the suspect actually planted an IED, and yet, an IED was found. As a result, following this case closely to ensure that there are no loose ends would be worthwhile, as this had the potential to be one of the largest terror attacks in the modern era, and very few details remain confirmed.
