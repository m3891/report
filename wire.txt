-International Events-

Europe: Wartime readiness plans and NATO exercises remain constant throughout the region. In the United Kingdom, roughly 100,000 reservists and former service members were sent letters by the British government this week, urging them to either reenlist or prepare for the U.K. to be involved in a conflict. In Poland, the deployment of roughly 15,000 troops to their border with Belarus and Ukraine is expected to take place over the coming weeks, as Poland seeks to bolster defenses in the region due to Russian missile attacks. Poland is also enacting a no-fly-zone along their border with Lithuania, due to the intensification of drone incursions along the Suwalki Gap.

-HomeFront-

USA: Cyberattacks and data breaches continue as before. Overnight 1Password, the password management software company reported a data breach which resulted in the leak of customer email addresses and payment details. 1Password states that no customer's passwords were breached, however malign actors gained access to an employee's corporate GitHub account, which allowed for the injection of malicious code into their systems.

American South: Severe weather systems swept through the region early Thanksgiving morning, with many states reporting large-scale power outages due to heavy rains and high winds. North Carolina and Virginia have reported over 130,000 residents without power, and Missouri has also reported over 70,000 residents without power.

New York: Last night Air Traffic Control operators at LaGuardia Airport issued a MANPAD alert for aircraft in the vicinity of the airport. The warning was issued after ATC was notified of a potential Man-Portable Air Defense (MANPAD) system that was possibly located to the east of the airport. After some time, the report was deemed to be not credible, and operations resumed as normal after a brief period of confusion among pilots in the sector. No further information was provided on what caused the initial report, or why the threat warning was issued.

-----END TEARLINE-----

Analyst Comment: While the MANPAD threat in New York was quickly deemed to be not credible, this is the second time this type of incident has been noted at this airport. A nearly identical threat report was issued at LaGuardia back in July, and that initial incident was quickly forgotten as no additional information was provided by officials regarding what that first case was all about.

One of the largest unknowns is exactly what the plan is for this scenario, or what the criteria for "MANPAD alert" actually is. There are no feasible ways to detect a shoulder-fired missile launcher via electronic surveillance means, so whatever this response plan is, it's probably built around Human Intelligence (HUMINT), or a phoned in threat.

ATC operators clearly have a procedure for an aircraft potentially being targeted by Surface-to-Air missiles, however it might be a good idea to convey that procedure to pilots. As it stands, commercial airline pilots have literally no recourse or countermeasure for this situation, so ATC casually telling pilots they may be shot down...but also not closing the airspace or issuing a ground stop...is rather suboptimal. This has happened twice now, and pilots were just as surprised and confused as the first time, and did not really know how to process what they were supposed to do. The ATC operators themselves were probably just as confused, having been alerted to the threat by outside agencies. As a result, it would be wise for some form of guidance to be issued by the FAA regarding what to do in these situations, especially if the threat of shootdowns within the homeland is now a threat profile that is being seriously considered by counterterrorism agencies.
